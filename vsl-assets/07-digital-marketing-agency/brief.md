## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The boutique agency owner:** Five to fifteen clients on retainers, still involved in delivery, and does finance at month end.
- **The freelancer turning agency:** Recently hired help and needs to see whether each client still pays after contractor costs.
- **The operations lead:** Tracks pipeline, retainers and receivables for the founder.
- **The specialist agency:** Sells a few core services and wants to know which one is most profitable.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Clients that cost more than they pay:** Scope creep and extra revisions can make a retainer unprofitable without anyone noticing.
- **Retainers that quietly end:** A retainer reaching its end date without a renewal conversation can simply stop.
- **Pipeline guesswork:** Without tracking leads and proposals, next quarter's revenue is a guess.
- **Service profitability unknown:** Agencies often do not know which service line earns the most after delivery costs.
- **Late-paying clients:** Overdue invoices strain cash when contractors and tools must be paid on time.
- **Tax and admin backlog:** Software subscriptions, contractors and expenses pile up for the accountant.

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Monthly recurring revenue (MRR):** Retainer revenue that repeats each month.
- **Client profitability:** What each client earns after the cost of delivering their work.
- **Average retainer:** Typical monthly value of a client retainer.
- **Pipeline value:** The value of open proposals and leads.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money clients owe on invoices already sent.
- **Net margin:** What the agency keeps after all costs, as a share of revenue.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "Our project tool already tracks this." — Project tools track tasks and time. This workbook is the owner's financial view: client and service profitability, retainers, pipeline, receivables, cash flow and tax set-aside. If you already have that, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The dashboards and formulas are built; you log revenue and costs. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "We are too small." — Essential covers the core: client revenue, expenses, a dashboard, a health snapshot and tax set-aside. Elite is for agencies managing pipelines, retainers and several services.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (8 sheets) covers client revenue, expenses, tax set-aside, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite (26 sheets) adds leads, sales pipeline, retainers, projects, service and client profitability, receivables, cash flow and forecasting.
- "Will it get us more clients?" — No. It shows your numbers so you can decide which clients and services to focus on.
- "Is it software we have to set up and maintain?" — No. It is a downloadable Excel workbook for the agency's numbers. It is not an AI or automation tool; you enter your data and the reports are built in.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
