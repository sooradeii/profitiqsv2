## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The owner-electrician:** Still on the tools most days, quotes jobs from experience and invoices at night.
- **The growing service shop:** Several electricians, a mix of service calls and projects, and no side-by-side view of who produces what.
- **The project-focused contractor:** Bids larger jobs and needs to know which ones actually pay.
- **The office manager:** Handles invoicing and receivables and wants a clear monthly picture.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Jobs that look good until materials land:** Material and labor costs arriving after the quote can erase a job's margin.
- **Electrician output is a guess:** Owners know who feels fastest, not whose hours produce the most revenue.
- **Leads that never turn into jobs:** Without tracking leads, it is hard to see which sources and follow-ups work.
- **Overdue invoices:** Receivables aging past 30, 60 or 90 days strain cash when payroll and suppliers are due.
- **No view of which customers pay off:** Some customers bring steady, profitable work; others do not.
- **Tax-time paperwork:** Materials, vans, tools and payroll costs end up scattered by year end.

## Common mistakes this audience makes (good cold-open material)

- Quoting from memory without checking past job costs
- Judging electricians on how busy they seem
- Not following up on leads
- Letting invoices age past 60 days without a process
- Treating every customer as equally profitable
- Buying materials without tracking them to jobs
- Keeping receipts in the van until tax season
- Not setting money aside for taxes

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Job margin:** What a job keeps after materials and labor.
- **Revenue per electrician:** How much billed work each electrician produces.
- **Lead conversion:** The share of leads that become paid jobs.
- **AR aging:** Unpaid invoices grouped by how long they have been outstanding.
- **Average job value:** Typical revenue per job.
- **Cash flow:** Money in and out each month, which can differ from profit when customers pay late.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My field service app already has reports." — Field apps dispatch and invoice. This workbook is the owner's view of job profit, electrician performance, leads, receivables aging, cash flow and tax set-aside. If you already see that, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The dashboards and formulas are built; you log jobs and expenses. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "I am a one-person shop." — Essential is built for the core numbers: jobs, expenses, a dashboard, a health snapshot and tax set-aside.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (8 sheets) covers job revenue, expenses, tax set-aside, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite (24 sheets) adds leads, job management, electrician performance, customers, AR aging, cash flow, KPIs, forecasting and benchmarking.
- "Will it make my jobs more profitable?" — No tool can promise that. It shows job costs and margins so you can quote and manage with real numbers.
- "Does it dispatch or invoice?" — No. It is an analysis workbook, not field service software.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
