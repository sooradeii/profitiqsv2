## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The solo owner-operator:** Drives the truck, books the loads, and does the bookkeeping at a truck stop or kitchen table at night.
- **The small fleet owner:** Owns two to five trucks, hires drivers, and needs each truck's cost per mile and margin compared side by side.
- **The leased-on operator:** Owns the truck but runs under a carrier's authority, with lease deductions and settlement statements that make true cost per mile hard to see.
- **The newly independent operator:** Recently got their own authority after driving for someone else and is learning the real cost of running a truck instead of just a paycheck.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Revenue looks good, margin does not:** A truck can gross a quarter million a year and still leave less take-home than expected once every real cost is counted.
- **Cost per mile is a guess:** Without fuel, maintenance, insurance and payments tracked against miles driven, it is hard to know the number that actually decides whether a load is worth taking.
- **Load profitability varies by lane and broker:** Two loads with the same rate on the confirmation can leave very different money once deadhead miles, fuel burn and broker fees are counted.
- **Fuel is the cost that moves the most:** Diesel prices swing week to week, and fuel is usually the single biggest variable expense on the truck.
- **Owner pay treated like it is free:** Many owner-operators do not count their own time behind the wheel as a real labor cost, which makes the business look more profitable than it is.
- **Tax time with a glovebox of receipts:** Per diem, depreciation, fuel records and deductions are hard to reconstruct in April from a glovebox and a shoebox.

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Cost per mile:** What it actually costs to run the truck one mile, counting fuel, maintenance, insurance, payments and the owner's own pay.
- **Revenue per mile:** What a load pays per mile driven, before costs are counted.
- **Load profitability:** What a load leaves after fuel, mileage cost and broker fees, not just the rate on the rate confirmation.
- **Operating margin:** What is left of revenue after every real operating cost, including the owner's own pay.
- **Fuel cost per mile:** Fuel spend divided by miles driven, tracked as diesel prices move.
- **Deadhead percentage:** The share of miles driven empty, with no paying load, between stops.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My ELD or load board already shows me loads and miles." — ELDs and load boards track dispatch and hours of service. This workbook adds cost per mile, load profitability, fuel intelligence and tax set-aside in one place. If your current tools already answer those, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The formulas and dashboards are already built; you enter loads and expenses. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "I only run one truck, this is overkill." — Essential is built for exactly that: load revenue, expenses, a dashboard and a health snapshot for a single truck. Elite adds load-by-load profitability, fuel intelligence and forecasting for operators who want to go deeper.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (9 sheets) covers load revenue, expenses, tax and deductions, a dashboard and a business health snapshot. Elite (26 sheets) adds load profitability, fuel intelligence, mileage tracking, cash flow, AR aging, forecasting, benchmarking and a full tax center.
- "Will this get me better paying loads?" — No. It shows you the real cost and margin on the loads you already run, so you can decide which lanes and brokers to keep or drop.
- "Does it handle dispatching or IFTA filing?" — No. It is an analysis and record-keeping workbook, not dispatch or filing software.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
