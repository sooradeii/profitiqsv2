## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The owner-plumber:** Still runs service calls most days, quotes jobs from experience and does the books at night.
- **The growing service company:** Several technicians and trucks, a membership base, and no side-by-side view of performance.
- **The install-focused contractor:** Takes on repipes and larger install jobs and needs to know which ones actually pay.
- **The office or dispatch manager:** Handles dispatch, invoicing and collections and wants a clear monthly picture.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **A callback that eats the job:** A return visit to fix the same problem can erase the margin the original job was supposed to earn.
- **Service calls and installs blended together:** A drain clear and a repipe get averaged into one revenue number instead of being compared on margin.
- **Technician output is a guess:** Owners know who feels busy, not whose hours produce the most profitable work.
- **Membership revenue nobody is watching:** Service plans get sold once and then forgotten until a customer calls to cancel.
- **Collections on bigger jobs:** A repipe or remodel invoice sitting unpaid past 30 days strains cash while payroll and suppliers are still due.
- **Tax-time paperwork:** Parts, trucks, tools and payroll costs end up scattered by year end.

- See job-level cost and revenue across every plumbing call

## Common mistakes this audience makes (good cold-open material)

- Treating every callback as bad luck instead of a pattern
- Blending service call and install revenue into one number
- Judging technicians on how busy they seem
- Letting memberships lapse without a renewal reminder
- Quoting installs without checking past job costs
- Holding parts inventory without checking what moves
- Letting invoices on bigger jobs age past 30 days without a process
- Not setting money aside for taxes

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Callback rate:** The share of jobs that require a return visit to fix the same problem.
- **Job margin:** What a job keeps after labor and materials, separated by service call and install.
- **Revenue per technician:** How much billed work each technician produces.
- **Active memberships:** Customers on a current service plan, the base of recurring work.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money owed on invoices already sent, especially on larger install jobs.
- **Average job value:** Typical revenue per job, by service call versus install.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My dispatch software already has reports." — Dispatch software runs the calendar and the invoices. This workbook is the owner's view of job profit, callbacks, technician performance, memberships, receivables, cash flow and tax set-aside. If you already see all of that, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The dashboards and formulas are built; you log jobs, calls and expenses. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "I am a one-truck operation." — Essential is built for the core numbers: jobs, expenses, a dashboard, a health snapshot and tax set-aside.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (9 sheets) covers job revenue, expenses, tax set-aside, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite (51 sheets) adds dispatch, technician productivity and scorecards, callback tracking, estimating, memberships and recurring revenue, leads, collections, inventory and purchase orders, forecasting and benchmarking.
- "Will it stop my callbacks?" — No tool can promise that. It shows which jobs, technicians or service types generate the most callbacks so you can address the cause.
- "Does it dispatch my technicians?" — No. It is an analysis workbook, not dispatch or scheduling software.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
