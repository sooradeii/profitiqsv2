## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The owner-technician:** Runs service calls and installs, quotes replacements and does the books at night.
- **The growing service company:** Several technicians and trucks, a maintenance agreement base, and no side-by-side view of performance.
- **The install-focused contractor:** Lives on replacement and installation work and wants to see margins by job.
- **The office or service manager:** Tracks agreements, estimates and receivables for the owner.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Seasonal swings:** Peak seasons hide weak months; without a monthly view, the slow season is a surprise.
- **Service call margins unknown:** Labor and parts on each call decide profit, and they are rarely reviewed together.
- **Maintenance agreements lapsing:** Agreements that reach renewal without a reminder quietly lapse, along with the work they bring.
- **Estimates that go cold:** Replacement quotes that are never followed up do not get a no; they just go quiet.
- **Technician performance:** Owners know who feels efficient, not who produces the most profitable work.
- **Tax-time paperwork:** Equipment, parts, vehicles and payroll costs end up scattered by year end.

## Common mistakes this audience makes (good cold-open material)

- Judging the year by the busy season
- Letting maintenance agreements renew by accident
- Sending replacement quotes without following up
- Comparing technicians on gut feel
- Holding parts inventory without checking what moves
- Pricing service calls without checking margins
- Leaving receipts until tax season
- Not setting money aside for taxes

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Average ticket:** Typical revenue per service call.
- **Service call margin:** What service calls keep after labor and parts.
- **Active maintenance agreements:** Customers on a current agreement, the base of recurring work.
- **Estimate close rate:** The share of estimates that become jobs.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money owed on invoices already sent.
- **Monthly revenue trend:** Revenue month by month, which shows the seasonal pattern.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My dispatch software already has reports." — Dispatch tools run calls and invoices. This workbook is the owner's view across service calls, installations, agreements, technicians, inventory, receivables, cash flow and taxes. If you already see that, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The dashboards and formulas are built; you log calls, jobs and expenses. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "Data entry takes time." — Fair. Owners who already export from their field software will find it quicker; the Quick Start shows what to enter first.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (8 sheets) covers job revenue, expenses, tax set-aside, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite (30 sheets) adds leads, estimates, work orders, service calls, technicians, customers, maintenance agreements, installations, inventory, fleet and labor, cash flow, receivables and forecasting.
- "Will it get me through the slow season?" — No tool can promise that. It shows monthly numbers so you can plan for it.
- "Does it dispatch technicians?" — No. It is an analysis workbook, not dispatch software.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.

## Who this is NOT for (say so on camera, it builds trust)

- Contractors without desktop Microsoft Excel

- Large multi-branch companies needing real-time reporting
- Owners who will not log calls and jobs regularly
