## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The owner-operator:** Runs routes personally, sells contracts, and does the paperwork at night.
- **The multi-technician company:** Several technicians and routes, and no side-by-side view of route or technician profit.
- **The contract-heavy business:** Most revenue comes from recurring service contracts that need renewing.
- **The office manager:** Tracks contracts, receivables and chemical stock for the owner.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Routes that do not pay their way:** Drive time and stops vary, and some routes earn far less per hour than others.
- **Contracts lapsing at renewal:** Recurring contracts that reach their renewal date without contact can quietly end.
- **Technician performance unclear:** Owners know who seems efficient, not who produces the most profitable work.
- **Chemical inventory costs:** Products on the shelf and in trucks tie up cash and are easy to lose track of.
- **Unpaid invoices:** Commercial accounts paying late strain cash flow.
- **Tax-time paperwork:** Chemicals, vehicles, equipment and payroll costs are scattered by year end.

## Why they actually buy

- See which routes and contracts are profitable
- Keep contracts renewing
- Compare technicians fairly
- Control chemical inventory
- Be organized for the accountant

## Common mistakes this audience makes (good cold-open material)

- Treating every route as equally profitable
- Letting contracts renew by accident
- Comparing technicians on how busy they seem
- Buying chemicals without tracking what is used
- Waiting too long to chase commercial invoices
- Pricing services without checking drive time
- Leaving receipts until tax season
- Not setting money aside for taxes

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Revenue per route:** What each route brings in for the time it takes.
- **Contract renewals:** Service contracts coming up for renewal.
- **Revenue per technician:** How much billed work each technician produces.
- **Recurring revenue:** Revenue from contracts that repeats each month or quarter.
- **Chemical inventory value:** The cost value of products on hand.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money customers owe on invoices already sent.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My routing software already has reports." — Routing tools plan the day. This workbook is the owner's view of contracts, routes, technicians, chemical inventory, receivables, cash flow and taxes. If you already see that, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The dashboards and formulas are built; you log services and expenses. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "I only have a few routes." — Essential covers the core: services, expenses, a dashboard, a health snapshot and tax set-aside.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (8 sheets) covers service entry, expenses, tax set-aside, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite (25 sheets) adds customers, service contracts, a service call log, route management, technician performance, revenue analytics, chemical inventory, receivables, cash flow and forecasting.
- "Will it grow my customer base?" — No. It shows your numbers so you can decide which routes, contracts and services to focus on.
- "Does it plan my routes?" — No. It analyzes route profitability; it is not routing software.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.

## Who this is NOT for (say so on camera, it builds trust)

- Operators without desktop Microsoft Excel
- Anyone needing routing, scheduling or invoicing software
- Large multi-branch companies needing real-time reporting
- Owners who will not log services regularly
