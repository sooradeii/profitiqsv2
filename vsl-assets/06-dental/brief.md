## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The new practice owner:** Recently bought or opened a practice and wants a clear view of production, collections and costs.
- **The established solo practice:** Busy schedule, steady patients, but no single view of how providers, hygiene and procedures perform.
- **The practice manager:** Runs operations and reports to the dentist-owner every month.
- **The growth-minded group:** Two or more providers and wants to compare them and plan growth.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Production is not collections:** Work produced and money collected can drift apart through insurance delays and patient balances.
- **New patients and recalls slipping:** A practice can be busy while recall patients quietly stop coming back.
- **Case acceptance blind spots:** Treatment presented but not accepted is revenue that never shows up.
- **Provider and hygiene performance:** Without a side-by-side view, it is hard to compare providers fairly.
- **Insurance claims and collections:** Outstanding claims and patient balances tie up cash.
- **Tax and accounting paperwork:** Payroll, supplies, equipment and marketing costs need to be organized for the accountant.

## Common mistakes this audience makes (good cold-open material)

- Watching production and ignoring collections
- Assuming recall patients will rebook on their own
- Not tracking treatment that was presented but not accepted
- Comparing providers on gut feel
- Letting insurance claims age without follow-up
- Spending on marketing without tracking the return
- Leaving supply and equipment costs untracked
- Organizing paperwork only at tax time

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Production:** The value of dentistry performed in a period.
- **Collections:** Money actually received, from patients and insurers.
- **Collection rate:** Collections as a share of production.
- **Case acceptance:** The share of presented treatment that patients accept.
- **Recall rate:** How many patients return for their recall visits.
- **New patients:** How many new patients the practice adds each month.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My practice management software has reports." — Practice software runs scheduling, charting and billing. This workbook is an owner's business view across production, collections, providers, hygiene, payroll, supplies, marketing ROI and taxes. If your reports already cover that, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The dashboards and formulas are built; data is entered or pasted in. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "Is patient data safe?" — It is a local Excel file on your computer, not a cloud system. Keep patient-identifying information out of it unless your practice's own HIPAA compliance policy allows it — that is each practice's call, not something this workbook decides for you.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (8 sheets) covers revenue, expenses, tax set-aside, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite (42 sheets) adds patients, recalls, treatment and case acceptance, provider and hygiene productivity, procedure profitability, claims, collections, payroll, supplies, marketing ROI and forecasting.
- "Will it grow my practice?" — No tool can promise that. It shows the numbers so you can decide where to focus.
- "Does it connect to my practice software?" — No. It is an Excel workbook; data is entered or pasted in.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
