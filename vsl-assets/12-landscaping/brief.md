## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The owner-operator:** Still runs a crew most days, quotes jobs from experience and does the books at night.
- **The multi-crew maintenance company:** Runs several crews on recurring routes and needs to see crew productivity and job profit.
- **The design-build and hardscape contractor:** Takes on installs, hardscaping and one-off projects and needs job costing on materials-heavy work.
- **The seasonal operation:** Runs mowing and maintenance in season and snow or cleanup work in the off-season, and needs to see how the slow months affect cash flow.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Properties priced by feel:** Quotes based on habit can leave some properties barely covering crew time and drive time between stops.
- **Crew productivity is invisible:** Owners know which crew feels efficient, not which one covers the most route for the hours it works.
- **Recurring contracts at risk:** Maintenance contracts are the backbone of the season, and a quiet cancellation or non-renewal is easy to miss.
- **Equipment and fuel creep:** Mowers, trimmers, trucks and fuel costs add up between seasons without being noticed.
- **Seasonal revenue swings:** Revenue in peak mowing season can look nothing like revenue in the off-season, which makes cash flow hard to plan.
- **Tax-time paperwork:** Fuel receipts, payroll and equipment costs end up scattered by the end of the year.

## Common mistakes this audience makes (good cold-open material)

- Quoting every property the same way regardless of size or terrain
- Never comparing crews on output
- Assuming maintenance contracts will keep renewing on their own
- Letting equipment and fuel costs creep without checking
- Treating the off-season as a surprise instead of planning for it
- Waiting too long to chase unpaid commercial invoices
- Keeping receipts in the truck until tax season
- Not setting money aside for taxes during peak season

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Job profitability:** What a property or job leaves after crew labor, materials and equipment time.
- **Crew productivity:** How much billable work each crew completes for the hours it works.
- **Recurring contract revenue:** Revenue from maintenance contracts that repeats each billing cycle.
- **Equipment cost per hour:** What it actually costs to run a mower, trimmer or truck for an hour of work.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money customers owe for completed jobs.
- **Seasonal revenue swing:** How much revenue shifts between peak season and the slow months.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My route or scheduling app already tracks jobs." — Route apps run the calendar and the map. This workbook adds job profitability, crew productivity, recurring contract revenue, cash flow and tax set-aside. If your app already answers those, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The formulas and dashboards are built; you log jobs and expenses. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "I am just a one-truck operation." — Essential is designed for the core numbers: jobs, expenses, labor, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite is for owners managing crews and contracts.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (8 sheets) covers job revenue, expenses, labor, tax set-aside, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite (27 sheets) adds crew performance, equipment and vehicle tracking, customer and contract analytics, receivables, cash flow and forecasting.
- "Will it make my crews faster?" — No tool can promise that. It shows the numbers so you can decide what to price, keep or change.
- "Does it schedule my routes or dispatch crews?" — No. It is an analysis workbook, not routing or dispatch software.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
