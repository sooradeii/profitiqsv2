## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The owner-operator roofer:** Still runs the ladder most days, bids jobs from experience and does the books after dark.
- **The multi-crew roofing company:** Runs several crews across job sites and needs to see which crew produces the most profitable work.
- **The insurance-restoration contractor:** Works storm and insurance-claim jobs and needs to track collections that can take months longer than cash jobs.
- **The office manager:** Handles estimates, job costs and receivables and wants a clear monthly picture of the business.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Bids that miss the real cost:** Material and labor costs that land after the estimate can turn a bid that looked fine into a job that barely breaks even.
- **Leads that never become estimates:** A lead that never turns into a written estimate is marketing spend with nothing to show for it.
- **Crew output across job sites is a guess:** Running several crews on several roofs at once makes it hard to see which crew finishes faster and costs less per job.
- **Insurance claims that collect slowly:** Insurance-claim and financed jobs can sit in receivables for months while an adjuster or lender processes the payout.
- **Weather-driven revenue swings:** A strong roofing season can mask a slow one, and revenue that swings with the weather is easy to misread month to month.
- **Tax-time paperwork:** Material receipts, crew payroll and permit costs end up scattered by year end.

## Why they actually buy

- See which jobs and bids are actually profitable
- Compare crews on real output across job sites
- Collect on insurance and financed jobs faster
- Keep materials and labor costs under control
- Be organized for the accountant

## Common mistakes this audience makes (good cold-open material)

- Bidding from memory without checking past job costs
- Letting leads go cold before they become estimates
- Judging crews on how busy they look instead of what they bill
- Treating insurance-claim jobs like cash jobs for cash-flow purposes
- Letting materials get bought without tracking them to a job
- Ignoring receivables until a crew needs paying
- Keeping material receipts in the truck until tax season
- Not setting money aside for taxes

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Job margin:** What a job keeps after materials, labor and crew costs.
- **Bid accuracy:** How close the estimated cost came to what the job actually cost.
- **Lead conversion:** The share of leads that become signed estimates.
- **AR aging:** Unpaid invoices and insurance claims grouped by how long they have been outstanding.
- **Crew performance:** How much revenue and margin each crew produces across its job sites.
- **Cash flow:** Money in and out each month, which can lag profit when insurance claims take months to pay.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My roofing software already tracks bids and jobs." — Roofing production software builds the estimate and schedules the crew. This workbook is the owner's view of job costing, crew performance, AR aging, cash flow and tax set-aside. If your software already shows that, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The dashboards and formulas are built; you log jobs and expenses. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "I run one crew. I am too small for this." — Essential is built for the core numbers: job revenue, expenses, a dashboard, a health snapshot and tax set-aside. Elite is for owners managing crews and job sites.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (9 sheets) covers job revenue entry, expenses, tax and deductions, a dashboard and a business health snapshot. Elite (27 sheets) adds leads, estimates, job management, job costing, AR aging, crew performance, cash flow, forecasting and benchmarking.
- "Will it make my bids more accurate?" — No tool can promise that. It shows what a job actually cost against what it was bid, so you can price the next one with real numbers.
- "Does it build estimates or handle insurance claims?" — No. It is an analysis workbook, not estimating or claims-management software.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
