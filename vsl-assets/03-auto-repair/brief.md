## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The busy owner-operator:** Runs one shop, still turns wrenches or writes service some days, and does the books at night. Knows the shop is busy but not which work actually pays.
- **The growing multi-bay shop:** Six or more bays, several technicians and a service advisor. Needs to see technician output, bay use and parts margin without buying a full shop-management suite.
- **The numbers-curious manager:** A service or general manager who reports to an owner and wants a clean monthly review instead of building spreadsheets from scratch.
- **The fleet-account shop:** Services commercial or fleet vehicles alongside retail customers and wants fleet work tracked separately.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Busy bays, thin profit:** A shop can be full every day and still end the month with less than expected, because labor, parts and comebacks are rarely looked at side by side.
- **Parts margin leaks:** Parts priced on the fly, supplier costs that creep up and returns that never get credited make parts profit hard to see.
- **Comebacks and warranty work:** Repeat repairs eat bay time and technician hours that nobody bills, and they are easy to forget once the car leaves.
- **Technician output is a guess:** Owners often know who feels fastest, not whose hours actually produce the most labor sales.
- **Average repair order is a mystery:** Without tracking it, it is hard to tell whether the shop is growing by more cars or by better tickets.
- **Tax time scramble:** Expenses live in receipts, card statements and the shop system, so the accountant gets a shoebox instead of a summary.

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Average repair order (ARO):** Total revenue divided by the number of repair orders. A simple way to see whether tickets are growing.
- **Car count:** How many vehicles the shop serviced in a period. Paired with ARO it explains where revenue came from.
- **Gross margin:** What is left after parts cost, as a share of revenue. The Elite dashboard shows it against a planning target.
- **Technician productivity:** How much billable work each technician produces for the hours they are paid.
- **Bay utilization:** How much of the available bay time is actually used for paying work.
- **Comeback rate:** The share of jobs that return for the same problem. Every comeback costs bay time and technician hours.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My shop management software already has reports." — Many shop systems focus on writing ROs and invoicing. This workbook is for the owner-level view across labor, parts, technicians, bays, expenses and taxes in one place. If your current reports already answer those questions, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The formulas, dashboards and reports are already built. You enter repair orders and expenses, and each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It does require desktop Excel.
- "I do not have time to enter data twice." — That is a fair concern. The workbook needs repair orders and expenses entered (or pasted) in. Owners who already export data from their shop system will find it easier.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential covers repair orders, expenses, a dashboard, a health snapshot and tax set-aside. Elite is the full 41-sheet system, adding deeper analysis such as technicians, bays, parts, warranty, comebacks and fleet accounts. Complete includes both.
- "Will this make my shop more profitable?" — No tool can promise that. The workbook shows the numbers clearly; what changes is up to the decisions you make with them.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
- "Does it replace my accountant?" — No. It produces an accountant-ready summary and tax set-aside estimates for planning. Tax figures are estimates, not advice.
