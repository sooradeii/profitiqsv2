## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The contract manufacturer:** Makes products to other companies' specs and needs each production run costed against the order that paid for it.
- **The job shop:** Runs custom and small-batch work and prices each job separately, with margin that can swing from run to run.
- **The single-product producer:** Makes one defined product at volume and lives or dies on the cost of materials, labor and overhead in that one line.
- **The growing shop adding product lines:** Started with one product and is adding more, and needs to see which lines carry the business and which ones drain it.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Production cost found out too late:** Materials, labor and overhead on a run often get totaled up after the product has shipped, once the price was already set.
- **Margin that swings by product:** One product line can carry the shop while another barely covers its own materials, and without a per-product view it is hard to tell which is which.
- **Production orders and sales orders drifting apart:** What the floor is actually making and what customers actually ordered can quietly stop matching.
- **Inventory tying up cash:** Raw materials and finished goods sitting on the shelf are cash the shop cannot use for anything else.
- **Late-paying commercial customers:** Unpaid invoices on business accounts turn into cash-flow pressure fast.
- **Tax-time paperwork:** Material receipts, payroll and production records end up scattered by the time the accountant needs them.

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Cost per unit:** What one unit costs to make once materials, labor and overhead are added up.
- **Product margin:** What a product keeps after its true production cost, as a share of what it sold for.
- **Inventory on hand:** The value of raw materials and finished goods sitting in the shop, tying up cash until they sell.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money customers owe for orders already shipped.
- **Net margin:** What the business keeps after all costs, as a share of revenue.
- **Open order value:** The value of sales orders taken but not yet produced and shipped.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My accounting software already tracks this." — Accounting software records transactions. This workbook builds per-product cost, production orders, inventory, profitability, cash flow and tax set-aside from them. If your software already breaks out cost and margin by product, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The formulas and dashboards are built; you log production and expenses. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "We are too small for this." — Essential is designed for the core numbers: product setup, production entry, expenses, a dashboard, a health snapshot and tax and deductions. Elite is for shops that want cost and margin by product and by production order.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (10 sheets) covers product setup, production entry, expenses, tax and deductions, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite (29 sheets) adds the product master, production orders, sales orders, cost accounting, product profitability, inventory control, customer accounts, receivables, cash flow, forecasting and benchmarking.
- "Will it make my products more profitable?" — No tool can promise that. It shows cost and margin by product clearly so you can decide what to price, keep or drop.
- "Is it inventory or production-planning software?" — No. It is an owner's analysis workbook, not inventory management or production-scheduling software.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
