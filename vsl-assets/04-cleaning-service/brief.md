## Buyer profiles (who actually buys this)

- **The owner-cleaner:** Still cleans most days, quotes jobs from experience and does the admin at night.
- **The crew-based company:** Runs several crews and needs to see crew productivity and job profit.
- **The commercial contract cleaner:** Works on recurring contracts and wants recurring revenue and contract terms in view.
- **The growing residential service:** Has a regular customer base and wants to keep customers coming back.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Jobs priced by feel:** Quotes based on habit can leave some jobs barely covering crew time and supplies.
- **Crew productivity is invisible:** Owners know which crew feels efficient, not which one produces the most profitable hours.
- **Recurring revenue at risk:** Regular customers and contracts are the backbone, and a quiet cancellation is easy to miss.
- **Supplies and travel creep:** Supplies, fuel and travel time between jobs add up without being noticed.
- **Late-paying customers:** Unpaid invoices on commercial accounts turn into cash-flow pressure.
- **Tax-time paperwork:** Receipts, payroll and job records end up scattered by the end of the year.

## Common mistakes this audience makes (good cold-open material)

- Quoting every job the same way regardless of time and supplies
- Never comparing crews on output
- Assuming regular customers will always stay
- Letting supply costs creep without checking
- Waiting too long to chase unpaid commercial invoices
- Judging the month by how busy it was
- Keeping receipts in a drawer until tax time
- Not setting money aside for taxes

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Job profitability:** What each job leaves after crew time and supplies.
- **Crew productivity:** How much paid work each crew completes for the hours it works.
- **Recurring revenue:** Revenue from regular customers and contracts that repeats each month.
- **Customer retention:** How many customers keep booking over time.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money customers owe for completed jobs.
- **Expense ratio:** Expenses as a share of revenue.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "My scheduling app already tracks jobs." — Scheduling apps run the calendar. This workbook adds job profitability, crew productivity, recurring revenue, cash flow and tax set-aside. If your app already answers those, you may not need it.
- "I am not an Excel person." — The formulas and dashboards are built; you log jobs and expenses. Each edition includes a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "I am too small for this." — Essential is designed for the core numbers: jobs, expenses, a dashboard, a health snapshot and tax set-aside. Elite is for owners managing crews and contracts.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (8 sheets) covers job revenue, expenses, tax set-aside, a dashboard and a health snapshot. Elite (27 sheets) adds customers and retention, crews and productivity, job profitability, contracts, recurring revenue, receivables, cash flow and forecasting.
- "Will it win me more clients?" — No. It shows your numbers so you can decide what to price, keep or change.
- "Does it schedule my crews?" — No. It is an analysis workbook, not scheduling software.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
