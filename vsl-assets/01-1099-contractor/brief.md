## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Irregular income, unclear profit:** Payments arrive on different schedules from different clients, so it is hard to know what a month actually earned after expenses.
- **Not knowing which clients pay off:** A client who sends the most work is not always the one who earns the most once time and expenses are counted.
- **Quarterly estimated taxes:** Without employer withholding, contractors have to plan for estimated taxes themselves, and the bill can be a surprise.
- **Mileage and expenses left untracked:** Deductible expenses and mileage are easy to lose when they are not logged as they happen.
- **Unpaid invoices:** Invoices that slip past their due date quietly turn into a cash-flow problem.
- **Year-end scramble:** Gathering a year of income, expenses and mileage for the tax preparer takes days when nothing is organized.

## Common mistakes this audience makes (good cold-open material)

- Spending every payment as if it were all profit
- Forgetting to set money aside for quarterly estimated taxes
- Logging mileage from memory at the end of the year
- Treating the biggest client as the best client
- Letting unpaid invoices sit without following up
- Mixing business and personal spending without tracking
- Waiting until tax season to organize receipts
- Never reviewing which services earn the most

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **Net profit after expenses:** What is left from client payments once business expenses are taken out.
- **Client profitability:** How much each client earns you once the time and costs of their work are counted.
- **Estimated tax set-aside:** The share of profit to put aside for quarterly estimated taxes so the bill is not a surprise.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money clients owe you for invoices already sent.
- **Business mileage:** Miles driven for business, logged as you go for your tax preparer.
- **Cash flow:** Money coming in and going out month by month, which can differ from profit when clients pay late.

## Objections to pre-empt (and the honest answer)

- "I just use my bank app." — A bank app shows money in and out. This workbook adds client profitability, mileage, estimated-tax planning and a year-end package on top. If the bank app already answers those questions for you, you may not need it.
- "I am not a spreadsheet person." — The formulas and dashboards are already built; you log income, expenses and mileage. Each edition has a Field Guide and a Quick Start. It needs desktop Excel.
- "I already use accounting software." — Accounting software records transactions. This is an owner's analysis tool: client profitability, forecasting, tax planning. Some people use both; some only need one.
- "Which edition do I need?" — Essential (11 sheets) covers clients, revenue, expenses, mileage, client performance, tax set-aside and a dashboard. Elite (26 sheets) adds invoices, receivables, cash flow, forecasting, an estimated tax center, a quarterly tax planner and a year-end tax package.
- "Will it lower my taxes?" — No tool can promise that. It helps you track deductible expenses and plan estimated taxes; your tax preparer decides what applies to you.
- "Is it for US contractors only?" — It is built around US 1099 work and estimated taxes. Contractors elsewhere should check the Field Guide first.
- "Is it a subscription?" — No. It is a one-time Digistore24 purchase of a downloadable Excel product, not a recurring charge.
