- **The scaling portfolio investor:** Has grown past two or three properties and needs a portfolio-wide view instead of judging each one from memory.

## The real pain points (use these to open / agitate)

- **Cash flow that hides the real return:** Rent showing up in the account each month does not say what the cap rate or cash-on-cash return actually is.
- **One property is a guess inside a growing portfolio:** With several properties, it is easy to know the total and not know which one is actually dragging on returns.
- **Vacancy and turnover cost that goes unmeasured:** Empty days and the cost of turning a unit over eat into returns quietly, property by property.
- **DSCR nobody checked before the lender did:** Debt service coverage ratio decides a refinance or new loan, and many owners do not know their number until the bank runs it.
- **Maintenance and capital expenses blurred together:** A new water heater and a leaky faucet land in the same bucket, which hides what the property actually costs to run.
- **Tax season across every property at once:** Depreciation, deductions and expenses scattered across multiple properties turn tax prep into a scramble.

## What it tracks (real KPIs, for the proof/features section)

- **NOI (net operating income):** What a property earns from rent after operating expenses, before the mortgage payment.
- **Cap rate:** NOI divided by property value — the return a property produces on its own, independent of how it is financed.
- **Cash-on-cash return:** Annual cash flow divided by the actual cash invested — what the money you put in is returning this year.
- **DSCR (debt service coverage ratio):** NOI divided by the mortgage payment — the number a lender checks before approving a loan or refinance.
- **Vacancy rate:** The share of days a unit sat empty, which is the rent you did not collect.
- **Equity growth:** How much ownership stake has grown through loan paydown and appreciation across the portfolio.

- "Will it get me a better refinance rate?" — No. It calculates the cap rate, DSCR and equity numbers a lender looks at, so you can walk in knowing them. The rate itself is the lender's decision.
